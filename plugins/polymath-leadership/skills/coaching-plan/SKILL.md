# coaching-plan

> Growth is not a pep talk; it's a small number of deliberate reps with feedback, plus someone spending their capital to put the person in the room. Coach the skill; sponsor the opportunity.

## When to use

- Building a development plan for a report/mentee, or a report has asked "how do I get to the next level?".
- Prepping growth-focused 1:1s or supporting a promotion case.
- A workflow invokes `polymath-leadership:coaching-plan`.

Not this when: you are writing an evaluation of past performance (`polymath-leadership:perf-review`) or prepping a single 1:1 agenda (`one-on-one-prep`). This is the forward-looking growth plan.

## Inputs

- The person's current level/strengths, their aspiration, and the next-level expectations (ladder/rubric).
- Recent feedback and 1:1 themes.

## Procedure

1. **Anchor to expectations**: lay current demonstrated behavior next to the next-level rubric; the gap is the raw material. Be specific and evidence-based, not "be more strategic".
2. **Pick 1–2 growth areas, not ten**: focus beats breadth. Choose areas that are both high-leverage for the role and motivating for the person (their goals matter — a plan they don't own won't happen).
3. **Coaching vs directive**: where they can find the answer, ask questions (GROW: Goal, Reality, Options, Will) to build their judgment; where there's a clear skill/knowledge gap, teach/point directly. Name which mode per area.
4. **Concrete practice reps**: turn each growth area into specific, real opportunities (lead this design, run that incident review, write the RFC) with a stretch but achievable scope — growth happens on real work, not in the abstract.
5. **Sponsorship, not just mentoring**: identify where you (or others) spend political capital to put them in visible rooms / on visible work, and advocate when they're not present. Mentoring advises; sponsorship opens doors — name both.
6. **Feedback loop**: timely, specific, behavior-focused feedback on each rep; SBI (Situation-Behavior-Impact). Adjust as they progress.
7. **Cadence + ownership**: a check-in rhythm and who owns each action (mostly them, supported by you); revisit the plan as growth or goals shift.

## Output

- A one-page growth plan: the current-vs-next-level gap (evidenced), the 1–2 chosen growth areas, coaching mode + concrete practice opportunities per area, the sponsorship actions, the feedback approach, and the check-in cadence.

## Quality bar

- Growth areas are anchored to explicit next-level expectations, evidence-based, and limited to 1–2.
- Each area has a real practice opportunity, not abstract advice.
- Sponsorship (opening doors) is named separately from mentoring (advice).
- Ownership sits with the report, supported by a cadence and concrete feedback.
